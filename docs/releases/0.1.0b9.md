Window changes now switch the termination policy of the existing pulse rather than closing it and starting an initial PWM pause.

- Opening a window during heating retains the valve command and pulse origin. The timed limit is ten minutes from that original opening; an already longer pulse closes immediately. Plateau/reference/lag learning is suspended while exposed.
- Opening a window while the valve is closed preserves ordinary demand hysteresis and cooling readiness. No initial forty-minute delay is imposed.
- Timed closure starts at least forty minutes closed; subsequent pulses require current room demand. Delayed closures extend the pause to at least four times actual opening.
- Closing the window during heating restores ordinary temperature/plateau termination in place, with the original maximum-open limit and fresh post-closure plateau samples. Exposed pulses do not teach residual lag. Closing during a pause cancels its timer and resumes ordinary cooling checks.
- Existing cold-window closure, stale-data inhibition, ownership and restart passivity remain. In-flight actuator service calls reconcile window changes before deciding whether opening remains permitted.

Tests cover both window transitions during active service calls and live pulses, demand/cooling before the first PWM pulse, repeat gating, late exposure, continuous pulse timestamps, fresh-only plateau learning and the forty-minute pause.
